## User data, identity, and security

- Preserve local libraries, saved playlists, history, preferences, account credentials, Android UID, Android signing identity, and Windows DPAPI data. No uninstall or data clearing is allowed.
- Android verification is emulator-only. Use a dedicated emulator for fixture tests that replace queues or libraries. Never access a physical phone under the current instruction.
- Never put API tokens, refresh tokens, OAuth JSON, signing keys, resolved audio URLs, private paths, or personal library contents in logs, source control, or release assets.
- Firebase project **echo-85da4** is the optional account backend. The Firebase-generated client key is expected to be visible in the app/source and is restricted server-side to Identity Toolkit and Token Service. Do not rotate/delete it without an older-client migration plan. Historical usage was not audited.
- Owner-only authenticated rules protect cloud data. Never make Firebase data public or log credentials. Keep Firestore library sync separate from the Realtime Database connected-playback protocol.
- Firebase library uploads require explicit consent. Sync playlists, saved metadata, favorites, and bounded history only; exclude audio, credentials, local paths, signed URLs, and downloads. Sign-out retains the device-wide local library. Do not assume unseen stale snapshots cannot restore old deletions unless tombstone behavior is explicitly added and verified.
- Maintain GPL-3.0-or-later notices and full corresponding-source obligations, including Temurin/native dependency sources where applicable. Do not add a proprietary identity SDK or GitHub token.

## Playback, queue, and connected devices

- Android's EchoPlaybackService owns ExoPlayer and queue state. UI actions use MediaController/service commands; the service re-resolves stable IDs and does not accept UI playback URLs.
- Android and Windows have an inset floating player dock and a full player surface built from platform-local Compose functions and shared leaf controls. Both derive their display from a presentation state and dispatch semantic playback intents; do not duplicate target/track/progress derivation between mini and full player.
- Playback focus can be Local, one explicitly selected remote device, or the active Play together group. The Windows multi-player dock shows the focused player on the left and a true overlapping deck on the right when there is more than one eligible independent target. The front card is raised and shows device name, artwork, song, and playback state; exposed back-card tabs prioritize identifying device names. Selecting a card focuses that exact device session, and all main-player controls and song/playlist requests route to it. Cards are for selection/status only: favorite, playlist, and download actions belong to the focused full player. The deck hides for local-only playback. Play together and its members are excluded; its tinted player and controls stay separate. The full player can still list every peer, including group members with status. A newly online device never steals focus. If the focused device disconnects or changes session, keep it in a disabled reconnecting state and require fresh selection; never silently switch targets. A device can join at most one active group at a time. Play together lets the user choose a subset of two to eight total devices, including the coordinator; remaining online peers stay independent and available.
- Android compact player: use one compact Spotify-style two-row card for Local, remote, and Play together. The top row has 40 dp circular artwork, device identity (or `Together · N devices`), song title and artist on separate lines, and a 48 dp Play/Pause control. The bottom row has 48 dp Previous, the shared accessible `PlayerSeekBar`, and 48 dp Next; omit time labels in the dock. Target 112 dp baseline height, growing to about 134 dp at 1.5× font scale; exceptional readiness status may add its own scaled line. Keep a 320 dp dock usable with a visible 48 dp next-player peek. Keep the `PlayerDeckState` focus behavior and use the same `PlayerSeekBar` in the full player; its 48 dp touch target uses a 4 dp track and 12 dp thumb. Capture the Local window UID, exact remote device/session and revisions, or group ref/timeline sequence/occurrence at drag start; send one seek on release and reject stale targets. Disable paging while seeking; one target has no next-player peek. Group members stay out of independent entries and new peers never steal focus. Current implementation and emulator evidence are recorded separately in [LOGS.md](LOGS.md).
- Playing a song or playlist routes to the focused target. Remote queue requests carry bounded portable YouTube recording metadata and unique queue occurrence IDs, never local paths, audio URLs, or account data; receiver identity/session and queue revisions are checked before replacement. Local-only recordings cannot be sent to another device.
- Keep stable recording identity separate from queue occurrence identity. Preserve duplicates, current playback, actual upcoming traversal, repeat-all wrap, shuffle order, and stale-action/revision checks.
- Queue append/search-selected/fresh-shuffle requests are app-UID-only, bounded, and resolved before mutation. A fresh shuffle makes every occurrence reachable; do not retain stale Smart shuffle anchors after an idle/completed session.
- Selecting a song or playlist replaces the focused remote/group queue from that occurrence and starts it. Remote/group Add to queue and Play next remain unavailable until the protocol can preserve the current occurrence, position, and paused/playing state; never emulate append by replaying a replacement queue from zero. Remote queue views are read-only snapshots.
- Scrubbing a shared player previews locally and sends one seek when the gesture ends. Carry the group ref, timeline sequence, and queue occurrence captured when scrubbing began; reject the request if the shared timeline changed during the gesture. A device-targeted seek must retain its captured device/session too.
- The sleep timer is service-owned and uses a monotonic deadline. It pauses without clearing the queue, survives screen closure while the service is alive, and clears when the service is destroyed.
- Windows YouTube playback retries transient network/server failures after 1, 3, and 6 seconds, then backs off up to 60 seconds between further attempts. Retries retain the queue occurrence and position, stop on user pause/change or app quit, and skip known permanent HTTP/provider errors including rate limits; keep the error visible while retrying.
- Windows Settings control whether closing the main window hides Echo to the system tray or quits it; hidden playback and connected participation remain active until tray Quit. Android Settings provide artwork/fixed-accent and light/dark appearance choices. Android audio and connected-playback participation are owned by the MediaLibraryService after a foreground start; ordinary Activity backgrounding must not stop playback. If Keep Echo available is off, removing the app task stops the service after playback pauses or ends; resuming playback or reconnecting cancels pending shutdown. OS process/service termination still ends participation, and task-removal behavior needs emulator acceptance.
- Windows staging preview runs through the development-only `:desktop:runStage` JavaExec task; the packaged `MainKt` always launches production mode and ignores runtime properties that could enable Stage. Android Stage is the separate `stageDebug` build (`dev.echo.music.stage`) with its own app data and OAuth callback scheme. Both show a floating Admin action for adding, disconnecting/reconnecting, removing, and assigning simulated 20 ms, 500 ms, or variable network estimates to Android/laptop peers. Android reconnect creates a new session while preserving the simulated playhead. Each separate Stage library seeds three silent demo recordings and `Stage Mix`. Focused remote transport, playhead progression, read-only queue snapshots, song/playlist queue replacement, group membership, paused private-queue restore, and common future start timing run in an in-process simulator without Gmail, Firebase, RTDB, or audio output. Account entry/sync, connected participation, automatic discovery, and updater checks are disabled in Android Stage; Move remains unavailable there. Manual Search and ordinary local playback use their existing providers. Preset RTTs are simulated inputs, not delayed packets; Stage verifies local UI/state routing, not real network transport or acoustic Sync.
- Move transfers the current portable recording, position, and remaining traversal without losing duplicates. It must reject unsupported recordings clearly; never silently drop/substitute tracks or change saved playlists.
- Play together is for 2–8 total same-account devices, including the coordinator. Its compact player uses the common Local/remote/group two-row layout: 40 dp circular artwork, `Together · N devices` and title/artist above, then Previous, the shared 48 dp seek, and Next below; Play/Pause stays on the top row. The target is 112 dp baseline and about 134 dp at 1.5× font scale, with more height when exceptional readiness status appears. The group details panel lists each member's readiness, session-matched network RTT/jitter estimate, and one Sync now action. Label estimates as network timing, never as speaker/output delay. Start, Sync, and committed shared-queue replacement choose one server-time deadline from the slowest fresh cohort estimate: eight serial RTT control rounds plus jitter, clock uncertainty, the poll interval, and a safety margin, with a 1.5-second floor and an 8-second cap. Missing, stale, or warming-up timing uses a non-adaptive 8-second start. The coordinator projects its playing position to that deadline; paused playback stays fixed. Clients schedule against a monotonic local deadline and validate the exact group epoch, member session, timeline sequence, and queue occurrence before applying. A client arriving after the deadline catches up to the current shared playhead. This uses one common start instead of adding the slowest peer's raw ping as an extra delay to each device. It does not promise sample-accurate acoustic synchronization. Group queue replacement remains epoch/sequence guarded, preserves order and duplicate occurrences, prepares all members before commit, retains the old queue on precommit failure, and reports postcommit activation failures as partial/unknown. Independent queues restore paused after leaving/coordinator loss. It is not cross-account, audio relay, or OS output routing.
- Adaptive timing writes use the additive owner-authenticated `syncTimingV1` RTDB child. The complete reviewed RTDB rules file was deployed to project echo-85da4 on 2026-11-07 after owner authorization, and a live readback matched the candidate rules. Signed-in clients can now use adaptive timing when peers report fresh estimates; missing or warming-up estimates still use the 8-second fallback. Stage presets exercise the timing UI/state without deploying rules or simulating actual network packets.
- Connected playback starts automatically for signed-in clients in 0.2.7. Windows tray mode keeps the process and connected client alive when the close-to-tray setting is enabled; Android follows the Keep Echo available setting after task removal. Runtime acceptance is still required. Do not promise participation after the app process/service stops. The candidate's original Windows timeout and real-account cold-start recovery after the latest retry patch were not captured.
- On Windows, the installed application and a portable preview are separate. MSI program files live under %LOCALAPPDATA%\\EchoMusicDesktop; user library and DPAPI credentials live separately under %LOCALAPPDATA%\\EchoMusic. Relaunch the installed Start-menu app after installation and confirm its version.

## Accounts, sync, and music sources

- Google sign-in uses Android AccountManager/installed account provider or Windows browser PKCE, verified identity, and Firebase sessions. Keep consent separate from sign-in where data upload is involved. Legacy Drive snapshot data is not evidence that transfers or multi-device convergence work.
- Saved-playlist sync retains its established Firestore schema and merge contracts. Concurrent edits should preserve conflict copies, duplicates and order; local file associations remain local. Do not broaden cloud payloads casually.
- The owner has no Spotify Premium and wants subscription-free listening. Keep Spotify public share-link, local CSV/account-data JSON imports, and account-data ZIP history as metadata snapshots. Save ZIPs only in the platform-local encrypted archive store (Android Keystore / Windows DPAPI); never sync or log them. Do not resume Premium-dependent onboarding, promise automatic sync, call Spotify metadata audio, or use Spotify credentials/tokens for scraping.
- Public YouTube search and playback use pinned NewPipeExtractor with lazy URL resolution. Song search also accepts a direct supported YouTube video URL and resolves it to the same stable track identity as search results. Store stable video IDs/watch links only. Playback retries transient network/server failures after 1, 3, and 6 seconds, then indefinitely with a 60-second maximum delay; do not retry known permanent HTTP/provider restrictions or rate limits. Keep host validation, availability checks, and service limits; no DRM bypass, login-cookie import, paid/member content, or promise that every recording works.
- Public YouTube and Spotify playlist imports must show partial/unknown counts before confirmation and preserve song order and duplicate occurrences. Playlist replacement/matching uses captured IDs and atomic guarded writes. Ambiguous matches require manual choice.
- Android and Windows show a five-page first-run tutorial and retain its completion locally. Spotify account-data ZIPs can be imported from Settings, saved encrypted on the current device, browsed locally for listening history/saved artists/albums, and removed without changing the Echo library. Keep platform UI and archive-store runtime acceptance aligned before shipping.
- Android appearance settings include artwork-derived or fixed accent, Dark/Light brightness, and validated preset/custom hex accents. Windows appearance settings include Dark/Light theme, artwork-following or custom accent, the close-to-tray behavior switch, and playback shortcuts listed in Settings. Space and navigation/seek shortcuts act on the focused Local, remote, or group player; a stale/unavailable remote target must not fall back to Local. Held Space repeats must not toggle more than once before key release. Preferences are local to each platform profile.
- Windows enforces one running process per normalized library path, including aliases of the default path. Lock filenames use SHA-256 identities; Stage has a separate namespace and can run beside production.
- Commons results must retain license and creator attribution. Do not make arbitrary URL rewriting or claim provider artwork can add detail that the original lacks.

## Library, offline, history, and UI invariants

- Room v1→v2 history migration is non-destructive. Every database builder must register the migration. Preserve playlists, duplicate occurrences, likes, mappings, credentials, and old history.
- Listening-time events reflect actual playing time measured monotonically. Pauses/buffering do not count; errors are neutral; history preferences are bounded local ranking hints, not a trained or guaranteed recommender.
- Manual offline audio and automatic frequent-song caching remain separate opt-in paths, budgets, and lifecycle policies. Verify byte count/integrity, support cancellation/retry, keep files private, and never include audio or signed URLs in metadata backups.
- App update and song download transfers remain separate non-exported Android foreground services with progress/cancellation handling. Do not promise force-stop/reboot resume or silent install.
- Keep navigation, saveable queue occurrence keys, accessibility actions, and stale-action protection. In the Windows compact dock, show the right-side overlapping player-card deck only when another independent target exists; keep device selection and group controls in the full player instead of adding a permanent device rail.
- Desktop needs installed 64-bit VLC 3. Do not package or silently install it.
